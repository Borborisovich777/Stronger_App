# Prepared workout templates

The Template library appears next to Exercise library on Workout, with all 14 prepared sessions available from the first visit. Users browse the catalog, choose one session, customize it, and save an independent copy. New installations start with an empty saved-template list. Existing routines and workout history are not replaced.

## Programming choices

The exercise selection draws on the movement preferences in the Stronger backup: flat and incline presses, machine fly work, dips, pulldowns, T-bar rows, rear-delt work, cable and incline curls, leg machines, and calf raises. Personal weights, dates, identifiers, and workout records are not part of the public library. Ambiguous historical names are not reinterpreted. New templates use current catalog entries and do not merge old records.

Compound lifts generally come first, followed by accessories. A and B vary equipment, rep ranges, or movement emphasis. Shorter core sessions and excluded-by-default optional accessories reduce the need to delete exercises at the gym. Both calisthenics options use pull-up and dip bars; the foundations session emphasizes rows and push-ups, while the intermediate session uses strict pull-ups and dips.

ACSM’s 2026 update prioritizes regular training of all major muscle groups, gradual progression, and tailoring training to the goal. It does not require training to failure, complex periodization, or a particular equipment type for the average healthy adult. Around ten weekly sets per muscle group is a hypertrophy-oriented reference, not a minimum everyone must reach immediately. [ACSM 2026 summary](https://www.acsm.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Position-Stand-infographic.pdf).

The earlier ACSM progression guidance supports placing demanding multi-joint lifts early and taking longer rests for heavier work. Its load and rest guidance is used as background; the current 2026 position takes precedence. [ACSM progression position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/).

These exact sessions are original, adjustable applications of those principles, not routines tested as a package in a clinical trial. The “about two good reps left” cue, exact ranges, and two-session progression rule are practical starting choices rather than universal thresholds.

## Using the sessions

Choose sessions to fit a weekly plan, not a rotation through this whole library. For a simple starting schedule, alternate Full body A and B on 2–3 nonconsecutive days. Body-part sessions are options within a broader split; chest and back work already involve arms and shoulders, so adjust accessory volume. Aim to cover all major muscle groups at least twice weekly as recovery allows.

Warm up with easy movement and lighter practice sets before your first loaded lifts. Choose a load or variation that leaves about 2 good reps in reserve; start easier while learning. When all sets reach the top of the range with controlled technique for two sessions, use the smallest practical load increase or a slightly harder variation and return to the lower end. For holds, build time before difficulty. These are working sets; warm-ups are additional.

Choose your starting weights in the editor. Zero is an unset external load; for bodyweight exercises, zero added load means bodyweight only. Stronger can prefill previous results for the same exercise and measurement type when you start. Check those values against today's rep range. Equipment changes need their own loads.

For the existing four-day body-part preference, choose one A/B variant per body-part session and consider total weekly coverage. Four isolated sessions do not automatically train every muscle twice; the full-body pair offers a simpler way to increase frequency. Arms and shoulders overlap with pressing and pulling, so these templates are alternatives within a plan, not extra sessions to stack on every workout.

“Optional” means excluded unless selected before making a copy. Selecting an alternative retains that exercise’s own catalog identity and measurement type. Review its required equipment and load. In an active workout, an occasional skip remains unchecked; completed sets continue to count normally.

Rep targets in the editor start at the lower end of the displayed range. Full ranges, side instructions, and progression guidance are saved as editable notes and carried into workouts. Timed holds use seconds, not fake reps. For a per-side movement, do both sides before marking the set done.

Estimated durations include warm-up, normal rest, and setup; optional work and slower setup can take longer. These templates assume generally healthy adults; adapt movements and load to ability, and stop movements that cause pain.

Strength emphasis · Some lifting experience · approximately 35–45 minutes

Default equipment: Barbell, bench, dumbbells, pec deck.

A main bench press, an incline press, and a fly. A stable press-first session with an optional dip finish.

Use a spotter or correctly positioned safeties for barbell bench. The optional dips are extra chest and triceps work; leave them out if pressing is already enough today.

## Implementation and review checks

- Existing data remains format version 1. Optional text notes on routines and routine exercises are validated and round-trip through backups.
- New installations and explicit resets start with an empty saved-template list; the bundled catalog remains available. Existing users keep their saved data, including any prepared sessions already saved by earlier versions.
- Browsing and customizing make no changes until Save template; saving adds a new routine with fresh IDs.
- Optional exercises are excluded by default. Alternative selection does not transfer load or history across exercise identities.
- The local ?preview=fresh page shows the first-launch home screen; ?preview=templates opens the catalog. Both use temporary state and never read or write workout storage. Reloading resets these previews.
- The local ?preview=existing page demonstrates browsing and saving an individual template alongside an existing routine and workout history, using temporary state only.
